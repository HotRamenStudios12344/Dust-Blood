<h1>Dust &amp; Blood</h1><p class="lead">A traditional Western tabletop roleplaying game about hard choices, difficult journeys, and the lives people build on the frontier.</p>
<p>This reference organizes the rules text currently available in this conversation into a clean, searchable handbook. Use the sidebar to browse sections or search for a term from the rules.</p>

<h1>Core Scores &amp; Health</h1><p class="lead">Every character has six Core Scores. Add the corresponding modifier to d20 rolls that use that score.</p>
<h2 id="scores">The Six Core Scores</h2><table><thead><tr><th>Score</th><th>Meaning</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Might</b></td><td>Physical power and strength.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Agility</b></td><td>Speed, coordination, reflexes, and precision.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Condition</b></td><td>Physical toughness and endurance.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Intelligence</b></td><td>Reasoning, knowledge, education, and problem-solving.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Wisdom</b></td><td>Perception, judgment, instincts, and awareness.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Charisma</b></td><td>Personality, influence, confidence, and social ability.</td></tr></tbody></table>
<h2 id="rolling">Rolling Core Scores</h2><ol><li>Roll 4d6 for one score.</li><li>Discard the lowest die and add the remaining three dice.</li><li>Repeat six times.</li><li>Assign the six results to your Core Scores in any order.</li></ol>
<h2 id="modifiers">Core Score Modifiers</h2><table><thead><tr><th>Score</th><th>Modifier</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1</td><td>−5</td></tr><tr><td>2–3</td><td>−4</td></tr><tr><td>4–5</td><td>−3</td></tr><tr><td>6–7</td><td>−2</td></tr><tr><td>8–9</td><td>−1</td></tr><tr><td>10–11</td><td>+0</td></tr><tr><td>12–13</td><td>+1</td></tr><tr><td>14–15</td><td>+2</td></tr><tr><td>16–17</td><td>+3</td></tr><tr><td>18</td><td>+4</td></tr></tbody></table>
<h2 id="health">Health</h2><p>At character creation, starting Health is <b>10 + your Condition score</b>. Each time your character gains a level, they gain an additional <b>1d6 Health</b>, unless a Trait or another ability changes how Health is gained.</p>
<h2 id="defense">Defense Bonus (DB)</h2><p>Your Defense Bonus is determined by the Defense Points (DP) provided by equipped clothing. Add the DP of each equipped clothing item together to determine your DB.</p><div class="callout"><strong>Example:</strong> A traveler’s shirt provides 7 DP, pants provide 5 DP, and a hat provides 2 DP. Together, they provide DB 14.</div>

<h1>Proficiencies</h1><p class="lead">Proficiency represents training or specialized experience in a skill, tool, weapon, or saving throw.</p>
<h2 id="using">Using Proficiency</h2><p>When you attempt an action you are proficient in, add your Proficiency Bonus (PB) to the roll alongside the relevant Core Score modifier. For example, a Ranch Hand proficient in Animal Handling adds PB to checks involving handling or calming animals.</p><p>A character without the relevant proficiency still makes the check using the appropriate Core Score modifier, unless a specific rule says otherwise.</p>
<h2 id="bonus">Proficiency Bonus</h2><p>All characters begin with <b>PB +2</b>. The bonus represents overall experience and increases with progression; it is not determined by how many proficiencies a character has.</p><div class="callout"><strong>Important:</strong> Add PB only once per roll, even if more than one proficiency applies, unless a specific rule states otherwise.</div>
<h2 id="types">Types of Proficiency</h2><p>Proficiencies can cover skills, tools, weapons, or saving throws. Backgrounds grant the proficiencies listed in their entries. Other proficiencies may be developed through character advancement where permitted by the rules.</p>

<h1>Character Creation</h1><p class="lead">Build a character through ability scores, Background, equipment, Traits, and Flaws.</p><h2 id="steps">Creation Steps</h2><ol><li>Roll six Core Scores using 4d6, dropping the lowest die each time, then assign the results in any order.</li><li>Calculate the modifier for each Core Score.</li><li>Choose a Background and record its Proficiencies, capabilities, drawbacks, and any starting Reputation or debt.</li><li>Choose the Background starting kit or starting money.</li><li>Calculate starting Health as 10 + Condition.</li><li>Calculate DB from the Defense Points of equipped clothing.</li><li>Record starting PB +2.</li><li>Choose Traits and Flaws using the Character Point rules supplied by the game.</li><li>Record alignment if required by the chosen Background.</li></ol>
<h2 id="cp">Character Points</h2><p>Dust &amp; Blood uses a classless Trait and Flaw system. Traits cost CP and may have multiple ranks; Flaws grant CP in exchange for meaningful disadvantages. Additional CP gained at later levels can be spent on character options allowed by the rules.</p><h2 id="rank-limits">Trait Rank Limits</h2><p>Ranks must be purchased in order: Rank I before Rank II, and Rank II before Rank III. At Level 1, only Trait Ranks I and II are available. Rank III becomes available at Level 5.</p><h2 id="choice">Meaningful Trade-offs</h2><p>Some Traits may cap or limit other Traits. For example, the supplied draft gives Sprinter II as an example that might cap Sneak at Rank I. These limits encourage specialization rather than allowing every character to master everything.</p>

title: Reputation

[HTML]
<h1>Reputation</h1><p class="lead">Reputation tracks how NPCs perceive and respond to a character. It represents public perception, not necessarily the truth about a character.</p><h2 id="range">Range</h2><p>Reputation ranges from <b>−20 to +20</b>. Characters normally start at 0 unless a Background or Flaw changes it. An Outlaw starts at −5.</p><h2 id="negative">Negative Reputation</h2><p>As Reputation becomes more negative, NPCs may become suspicious, hostile, or unwilling to associate with the character. They might refuse service, deny help, or alert authorities.</p><p>At −20, a character is likely notorious nationwide for extraordinarily infamous actions. Reaching that level requires extreme notoriety; ordinary crimes and everyday misbehavior are not enough.</p><h2 id="positive">Positive Reputation</h2><p>As Reputation becomes more positive, NPCs are increasingly likely to trust, respect, and assist the character. High Reputation may provide better deals, opportunities, and cooperation. At +20, a character is exceptionally respected and may be famous nationwide for extraordinary deeds.</p><h2 id="changing">Changing Reputation</h2><p>Reputation changes through actions, accomplishments, and what other people learn about the character. The Game Master decides how widely events are known and how they affect public standing.</p>

<h1>Action Points &amp; Turns</h1><p class="lead">Action Points (AP) represent a character’s capacity to act during a combat round.</p><h2 id="refresh">Regaining AP</h2><ul><li>At the start of your active turn, regenerate <b>3 AP</b>.</li><li>You may save unused AP, up to a maximum of <b>12 saved AP</b>.</li></ul><h2 id="movement">Movement</h2><p>Moving on foot costs <b>1 AP per 5 feet</b>.</p><h2 id="live-combat">Live Combat</h2><p>Under the Live Combat rule, saved AP can be spent on an inactive turn to move, take cover, reload, or interact. Saved AP cannot be used to make firearm attacks during another character’s turn.</p><h2 id="other-actions">Actions and turns</h2><p>Specific action costs and combat resolution depend on the individual action rules. Use the relevant entry in the full manuscript for details not included in the text available here.</p>

<h1>Mounts</h1>

<p>Mounts are a big part of <em>Dust &amp; Blood</em>, but live mounts such as oxen and horses require their own sheet.</p>

<h1>Horses</h1>

<h2>1. Horse Creation</h2>
<p>Create a horse using these steps:</p>
<ol>
<li>Choose a breed and apply its attribute bonuses.</li>
<li>Roll 1d6 for individual quality.</li>
<li>Choose or roll its temperament.</li>
<li>Calculate HP, DB, speed, stamina, and carrying capacity.</li>
<li>Choose its training.</li>
<li>Record its identification, equipment, and price.</li>
</ol>
<p>Horses use the same six attributes and modifiers as characters: Might, Agility, Condition, Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma.</p>

<h3>Individual Quality</h3>
<p>Roll 1d6 after applying breed bonuses.</p>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Roll</th><th>Effect</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>1</td><td>Primary attribute -1</td></tr>
<tr><td>2</td><td>Secondary attribute -1</td></tr>
<tr><td>3–4</td><td>No change</td></tr>
<tr><td>5</td><td>Primary attribute +1</td></tr>
<tr><td>6</td><td>Primary attribute +1 and secondary attribute +1</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The GM determines which attributes are primary and secondary for each breed. This roll can change each attribute by no more than 1.</p>

<h2>2. Horse Breeds</h2>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Breed</th><th>Attribute Bonuses</th><th>Specialization</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Quarter Horse</td><td>Might +1, Agility +1, Condition +1</td><td>Ranch work</td></tr>
<tr><td>Thoroughbred</td><td>Agility +2, Condition +1</td><td>Speed and pursuit</td></tr>
<tr><td>Paint Horse</td><td>Agility +1, Condition +1, Wisdom +1</td><td>General riding and stock work</td></tr>
<tr><td>Saddlebred</td><td>Agility +2, Charisma +1</td><td>Responsive riding</td></tr>
<tr><td>Draft Horse</td><td>Might +2, Condition +2, Wisdom +1</td><td>Heavy hauling</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Breed bonuses represent natural abilities, not training.</p>

<h2>3. Horse Statistics</h2>

<h3>Hit Points (HP)</h3>
<p><strong>HP = 10 + Condition modifier + Breed HP bonus</strong></p>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Horse Type</th><th>HP Bonus</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Light riding horse</td><td>+0</td></tr>
<tr><td>Standard riding horse</td><td>+2</td></tr>
<tr><td>Heavy riding horse</td><td>+3</td></tr>
<tr><td>Draft horse</td><td>+5</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>At 0 HP, a horse is incapacitated and cannot be ridden or work normally.</p>

<h3>Defense Bonus (DB)</h3>
<p><strong>DB = 10 + Agility modifier + Armor DP</strong></p>
<p>Armor DP comes from protective equipment; ordinary saddles and bridles provide none.</p>

<h3>Speed</h3>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Gait</th><th>Base Speed</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Walking</td><td>20 ft./round</td></tr>
<tr><td>Trotting</td><td>30 ft./round</td></tr>
<tr><td>Cantering</td><td>40 ft./round</td></tr>
<tr><td>Galloping</td><td>60 ft./round</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<ul>
<li><strong>Thoroughbred:</strong> +10 ft. to cantering and galloping.</li>
<li><strong>Draft horse:</strong> -10 ft. to cantering and galloping.</li>
<li><strong>Other breeds:</strong> No change.</li>
</ul>
<p>Extended fast travel may require stamina checks.</p>

<h3>Stamina</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Maximum Stamina:</strong> 6 + Condition modifier.</li>
<li><strong>Strenuous travel or heavy labor:</strong> -1 Stamina per hour.</li>
<li><strong>Galloping for a full minute:</strong> An additional -1 Stamina.</li>
<li><strong>At 0 Stamina:</strong> Cannot gallop and suffers -2 to physical checks.</li>
<li><strong>Rest:</strong> Recover 2 Stamina per hour with water and reasonable conditions.</li>
<li><strong>Full night's rest:</strong> Restore all Stamina with adequate food, water, and shelter.</li>
</ul>
<p>The GM may adjust costs for terrain, weather, workload, and condition.</p>

<h2>6. Riding and Handling</h2>
<p>When a task is difficult or dangerous, the GM may call for an Animal Handling check using the normal check rules.</p>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Task</th><th>Difficulty</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Mount a calm, trained horse</td><td>5</td></tr>
<tr><td>Control ordinary travel</td><td>8</td></tr>
<tr><td>Navigate difficult terrain</td><td>10</td></tr>
<tr><td>Calm a frightened horse</td><td>12</td></tr>
<tr><td>Ride through nearby gunfire</td><td>15</td></tr>
<tr><td>Control an unfamiliar, panicking horse</td><td>18</td></tr>
<tr><td>Perform an exceptional maneuver</td><td>20</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>These are suggested difficulties. Failure does not automatically cause a fall; consequences depend on the situation.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Mounting:</strong> No check for a cooperative horse under normal conditions.</li>
<li><strong>Dismounting:</strong> No check under normal conditions.</li>
<li><strong>Riding armed:</strong> Difficult shots or riding at a gallop may require checks or penalties.</li>
<li><strong>Falling:</strong> The GM may call for an Agility check to land safely; failure uses normal falling-damage rules.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Fear and Panic</h3>
<p>Gunfire, predators, fires, injuries, and sudden threats may trigger a Wisdom-based check using the horse's Wisdom modifier.</p>
<p>On success, the horse remains under control. On failure, it may hesitate, flee, rear, or resist commands. Temperament, training, and threat severity affect the outcome. Appropriate combat training may grant a bonus against gunfire-related fear.</p>

<h2>10. Horse Core Rules</h2>
<ul>
<li>Horses use the same six attributes and modifier rules as characters.</li>
<li>Breed determines natural specialization; individual quality distinguishes horses of the same breed.</li>
<li>Temperament affects behavior, while training determines reliable capabilities.</li>
<li>HP, DB, speed, stamina, and carrying capacity determine physical performance.</li>
<li>Equipment is separate unless included in the purchase price.</li>
<li>The GM determines when checks are required and what failure means.</li>
<li>Horses can become frightened, exhausted, injured, or sick and require proper care.</li>
<li>All horse mechanics must remain compatible with the game's attributes, Action Points, combat, and equipment systems.</li>
</ul>

<h1>Oxen</h1>

<h2>1. Oxen Creation</h2>
<p>Create an ox using these steps:</p>
<ol>
<li>Choose a breed and apply its attribute bonuses.</li>
<li>Roll 1d6 for individual quality.</li>
<li>Choose or roll its temperament.</li>
<li>Calculate HP, DB, speed, stamina, and pulling capacity.</li>
<li>Record its training, identification, equipment, and price.</li>
</ol>
<p>Oxen use the same six attributes and modifiers as characters: Might, Agility, Condition, Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma.</p>
<p>Oxen are primarily working animals used for pulling wagons, plowing fields, and hauling heavy supplies. They can be trained to follow commands and work in teams but are not suitable for ordinary riding.</p>

<h3>Individual Quality</h3>
<p>Roll 1d6 after applying breed bonuses.</p>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Roll</th><th>Effect</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>1</td><td>Primary attribute -1</td></tr>
<tr><td>2</td><td>Secondary attribute -1</td></tr>
<tr><td>3–4</td><td>No change</td></tr>
<tr><td>5</td><td>Primary attribute +1</td></tr>
<tr><td>6</td><td>Primary attribute +1 and secondary attribute +1</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The GM determines primary and secondary attributes for each breed. Each attribute can change by no more than 1 from this roll.</p>

<h2>3. Ox Statistics</h2>

<h3>Hit Points (HP)</h3>
<p><strong>HP = 10 + Condition modifier + Size HP bonus</strong></p>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Size</th><th>HP Bonus</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Small</td><td>+0</td></tr>
<tr><td>Standard</td><td>+3</td></tr>
<tr><td>Large</td><td>+5</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>At 0 HP, an ox is incapacitated and cannot work normally.</p>

<h3>Defense Bonus (DB)</h3>
<p><strong>DB = 10 + Agility modifier + Armor DP</strong></p>
<p>Armor DP comes from protective equipment. Ordinary yokes and harnesses provide no armor.</p>

<h3>Speed</h3>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Movement</th><th>Speed</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Walking</td><td>20 ft./round</td></tr>
<tr><td>Working Pace</td><td>15 ft./round</td></tr>
<tr><td>Fast Walk</td><td>25 ft./round</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Oxen cannot gallop like horses. Their speed while pulling a load depends on the load, terrain, and condition of the animal.</p>

<h3>Stamina</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Maximum Stamina:</strong> 6 + Condition modifier.</li>
<li><strong>Heavy labor:</strong> -1 Stamina per hour.</li>
<li><strong>Sustained maximum-effort pulling:</strong> An additional -1 Stamina per hour.</li>
<li><strong>At 0 Stamina:</strong> The ox becomes exhausted, cannot perform heavy pulling, and suffers -2 to physical checks.</li>
<li><strong>Rest:</strong> Recover 2 Stamina per hour with water and reasonable conditions.</li>
<li><strong>Full night's rest:</strong> Restore all Stamina with adequate food, water, and shelter.</li>
</ul>
<p>The GM may adjust stamina costs for terrain, weather, and workload.</p>

<h3>Pulling Capacity</h3>
<p>Pulling capacity determines the weight an ox can move using a suitable yoke or harness. It represents the total load being pulled, not the weight carried on the animal's back.</p>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Might Modifier</th><th>Comfortable Pulling Load</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>-1 or lower</td><td>500 lb.</td></tr>
<tr><td>+0</td><td>750 lb.</td></tr>
<tr><td>+1</td><td>1,000 lb.</td></tr>
<tr><td>+2</td><td>1,250 lb.</td></tr>
<tr><td>+3</td><td>1,500 lb.</td></tr>
<tr><td>+4</td><td>1,750 lb.</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>These are provisional game-balance values, not real-world hauling recommendations.</p>
<ul>
<li>Difficult terrain, steep slopes, mud, and poor weather can reduce pulling capacity.</li>
<li>Loads above comfortable capacity require GM approval and increase Stamina expenditure.</li>
<li>An ox should not exceed 150% of its comfortable pulling capacity.</li>
<li>Two oxen working as a team can pull more than one, but their combined capacity depends on training, harnessing, and coordination.</li>
</ul>

<h1>Core Combat System</h1>

<h2>Core Resolution</h2>
<p><em>Dust &amp; Blood</em> uses a <strong>d20</strong> as its primary resolution die. When a character attempts something with a meaningful chance of success or failure, the player rolls a d20 and applies the appropriate modifiers. This system keeps gameplay simple and fast while allowing combat, skills, and other mechanics to remain flexible.</p>

<h2>Action Points (AP)</h2>
<p>The game uses an Action Point system to determine how much a character can do. AP can be spent on movement, drawing or holstering weapons, reloading, taking cover, interacting with objects, climbing, and other actions. Characters can also save unused AP for later turns.</p>

<h3>Turn Definitions</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Active Turn:</strong> The turn currently belonging to your character.</li>
<li><strong>Inactive Turn:</strong> Any turn belonging to another character.</li>
</ul>

<h3>AP Regeneration</h3>
<p>At the start of each active turn, a player regenerates <strong>3 AP</strong>. These AP are added to any AP the character has saved from previous turns; saved AP are not lost when new AP are gained. For example, if a character has 3 AP saved when their next active turn begins, they will have <strong>6 AP</strong> available. The absolute most a character can have at any given time is <strong>12 AP</strong>.</p>
<p>Characters regenerate AP even while unconscious. However, regenerating AP does not necessarily mean an unconscious character can spend those AP to perform actions.</p>

<h3>Live Combat</h3>
<p>Live Combat allows characters to spend their available AP outside their active turns. During a friend's turn or an enemy's turn, a character may spend AP to perform appropriate non-attacking actions, allowing them to react to changes on the battlefield rather than waiting passively for their next turn.</p>
<p>During an inactive turn, characters may move on foot or by mount, take cover, and interact with the world. <strong>Firearm attacks can only be made during a character's own active turn.</strong></p>

<h2>Actions</h2>
<p>Actions such as talking or activating Parley do not require AP. Keep in mind that the enemy may not care about your efforts at negotiation.</p>
<p>Actions that do require AP are usually performed during combat. These include movement, shooting, reloading, taking cover, sneaking, mounting or dismounting a horse, lassoing, and world interactions. Actions that require AP are not limited to combat; your GM may require AP during tense moments, puzzles, chases, and other situations.</p>

<h2>Firearms</h2>

<h3>Hit Rolls and Location Rolls</h3>
<p>Firearm attacks use two separate d20 rolls: a <strong>Hit Roll</strong> and a <strong>Location Roll</strong>. The Hit Roll determines whether your attack hits the target. If the Hit Roll succeeds, roll for the Location Roll to determine which body part is struck. If the Hit Roll fails, do not make a Location Roll.</p>

<h3>Hit Roll Modifier</h3>
<p>Calculate your Hit Roll modifier by subtracting 2 from your Agility modifier. Apply the resulting modifier to your Hit Roll: add it if positive or subtract it if negative. For example, an Agility modifier of +5 gives you a +3 Hit Roll modifier, while an Agility modifier of 0 gives you a -2 Hit Roll modifier.</p>

<h3>Firing</h3>
<p>Firing a firearm costs <strong>1 Action Point (AP)</strong>, regardless of whether the attack hits or misses.</p>

<h3>Reloading</h3>
<p>Reloading costs vary depending on the firearm. Consult <strong>WEAPONS AND ITEMS</strong> to find the specific AP cost required to reload your weapon.</p>

<h2>Hit Location</h2>
<p>To successfully hit a target with a firearm, your Hit Roll must meet or exceed the target's <strong>Defense Bonus (DB)</strong>. The DB functions similarly to Armor Class (AC) in Dungeons &amp; Dragons: it is the number you must roll on your d20 to successfully hit the target. If your Hit Roll equals or exceeds the target's DB, the attack hits, and you roll for hit location. If your Hit Roll is lower than the target's DB, the attack misses.</p>

<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Roll</th><th>Location</th><th>Effect</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>1</td><td>Head</td><td>The target is killed instantly, no matter the DB.</td></tr>
<tr><td>2–5</td><td>Arms</td><td>The target drops whatever they are holding and makes a DC 9 Condition Check. On a failure, the arm is incapacitated and cannot be used. On a success, the arm remains functional.</td></tr>
<tr><td>6–9</td><td>Legs</td><td>The target makes a DC 9 Condition Check. On a failure, the leg is incapacitated and the target's movement speed is halved. On a success, the leg remains functional and movement is unaffected.</td></tr>
<tr><td>10–20</td><td>Torso</td><td>Roll the weapon's damage normally.</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<p>The hit-location system is divided by function: the <strong>head is lethal, the torso deals damage, the arms impair utility, and the legs impair mobility.</strong></p>

<h2>Targeted Shots</h2>
<p>A character may declare a <strong>Targeted Shot</strong> before firing in order to attempt to hit a specific body part. A Targeted Shot only succeeds if the Hit Roll is a <strong>natural 20</strong>. If the Hit Roll is anything other than a natural 20, the shot fails and no Location Roll is made.</p>
<p>Targeted Shots cannot be declared while the character is riding a horse.</p>

<h2>Mounts and Firearms</h2>
<p>When firing a firearm while riding a horse, the attack is made with <strong>disadvantage</strong>.</p>
<p>A character riding a horse <strong>cannot declare a Targeted Shot</strong>. Mounted firearm attacks therefore rely on the normal Hit Roll and Location Roll system.</p>

<h2>Condition Checks</h2>
<p>The standard Condition Check difficulty is <strong>DC 5</strong>. Firearm injuries to the arms and legs specifically use <strong>DC 9</strong>.</p>
<p>For firearm injuries to the arms and legs, the Condition Check determines whether the injury causes its associated impairment. A successful check means the associated impairment does not occur.</p>

<h2>Knives</h2>
<p>Knives are primarily close-range weapons that provide an alternative to firearms. They are not intended to compete with firearms in raw damage, but instead offer a simple and versatile option for characters who choose not to rely on guns. Knives can also be useful for characters seeking a quieter way to eliminate an opponent when circumstances allow. Their primary combat utility is Release, which allows a character equipped with a knife to attempt to escape a grapple. Beyond this, knives remain relatively simple weapons, relying on close-range attacks rather than numerous specialized mechanics.</p>
<p>Knife attacks use the same Hit Roll system as firearms. If the Hit Roll succeeds, the attacker rolls a Location Roll to determine where the strike lands. The effects of the strike depend on the location rolled and the type of attack being performed.</p>

<h3>Knife Hit Locations</h3>
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Roll</th><th>Location</th><th>Effect</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>1–7 (35%)</td><td>Arms</td><td>The target drops whatever they are holding and makes a DC 9 Condition Check. On a failure, the struck arm is incapacitated and cannot be used. On a success, the arm remains functional.</td></tr>
<tr><td>8–14 (35%)</td><td>Legs</td><td>The target makes a DC 5 Condition Check. On a failure, the struck leg is incapacitated and the target's movement speed is halved. On a success, the leg remains functional and movement is unaffected.</td></tr>
<tr><td>15–20 (30%)</td><td>Torso</td><td>Apply damage according to the type of knife attack being performed.</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<h3>Slashing</h3>
<p>Slashing deals an initial <strong>1d4 damage</strong> and inflicts the Bleeding condition when it hits the torso. While the target is bleeding, they take an additional 1d4 damage at the start of each of their active turns until the wound is bandaged. This bleeding effect only applies to torso hits.</p>

<h3>Stabbing</h3>
<p>Stabbing deals greater initial damage of <strong>1d6</strong> but causes little to no recurring bleeding. Stabbing attacks only deal their damage when the Location Roll indicates a torso hit. If the roll indicates the arms or legs, the stabbing attack does not connect with the torso, and its normal stabbing damage is not applied. The Condition DC roll for stabbing attacks is <strong>DC 14</strong>.</p>

<h3>Slit Throat</h3>
<p>Knives also have a special action called Slit Throat. When sneaking behind someone, a character may declare that they are attempting to slit the target's throat. This action succeeds only on a natural 20 on the Hit Roll and serves as the knife equivalent of a firearm headshot. The action can be heard by anyone within a 10-foot radius. Because this is a declared action, the attacker must announce their intention to attempt it before rolling.</p>

<h3>Release</h3>
<p>Knives also have a Release function that allows a character to cut themselves free from a lasso without spending AP. Instead of spending 3 AP to untangle themselves, a character equipped with a knife can attempt to cut the lasso and escape by succeeding on a <strong>DC 5 Condition Check</strong>. This action costs no AP.</p>

<h2>Grappling</h2>
<p>Grappling is divided into two types: <strong>Physical Grappling</strong> and <strong>Lasso Grappling</strong>.</p>

<h3>Physical Grappling</h3>
<p>A Physical Grapple represents directly controlling another character, such as tackling them or restraining their arms. To initiate a Physical Grapple, the attacker makes a Might Check against the target's Might, with a DC equal to the target's Might. If the attacker succeeds, the target becomes physically grappled.</p>
<p>To escape a Physical Grapple, compare the Might of both characters. If the grappled character has higher Might than the grappler, they automatically overcome the grapple. If their Might is equal to or lower than the grappler's, they must succeed on a DC 10 Might Check to escape.</p>

<h3>Lasso Grappling</h3>
<p>A Lasso Grapple costs <strong>2 AP</strong> and requires a Hit Roll against the target's DB. If the Hit Roll succeeds, both characters roll a d20 and add their Agility modifier. If the attacker's total is higher, the target becomes Lassoed. A Lassoed character cannot move freely.</p>
<p>A lassoed individual may use Release if they have a knife equipped, cutting the lasso and escaping. Without an equipped knife, they must spend <strong>3 AP</strong> and succeed on a Might Check against <strong>DC 15</strong> to escape. The attacker may spend 1 AP to pull the target 5 feet closer. The attacker does not need to physically hold the target to maintain the lasso.</p>

<h2>Punching and Physical Attacks</h2>
<p>Punching or making any other unarmed physical attack requires you to be within 5 feet of your opponent. To determine whether an attack hits, roll against the target's Defense Class (DC) divided by 2. Unarmed damage is calculated by dividing your Might score by the result of a 1d6 roll. Making one punch costs <strong>1 Action Point (AP)</strong>, and each punch requires its own attack roll and damage roll.</p>

<h2>Cover</h2>
<p>Cover in <em>Dust &amp; Blood</em> works a little differently than in most tabletop RPGs. Cover protects specific parts of a character's body rather than making them harder to hit overall.</p>
<p>When attacking an enemy behind cover, roll your <strong>Hit Roll</strong> normally. If the attack hits, roll for the <strong>Hit Location</strong> as usual. If the body part you rolled is protected by cover, the shot hits the cover instead, and the target takes no damage. If the body part is exposed, the attack hits the target normally.</p>

<h2>Gun Handling</h2>
<p>Every gun has a minimum required number of hands to use the weapon. Some traits will alter this; consult the weapon-type tables. For example, if you need two hands to use a weapon but want to climb a ladder or lead a horse, you cannot use that weapon while doing so.</p>
<p>When you go prone and mount your weapon against the ground, or mount it on a stable surface such as a windowsill, you gain <strong>+5 to Hit</strong>. This only works with rifles.</p>

<h2>Helping Hand</h2>
<p>When a character assists an ally with an Ability Check, the assisting character rolls 4d6. Convert the total rolled into its corresponding modifier using the same rules as character creation. Apply that modifier to the ally's Ability Check, adding it if positive or subtracting it if negative. A high roll can improve the ally's chances of success, while a low roll can hinder them.</p>

<h2>Proficiency in Weapons</h2>
<p>If you train in a weapon during downtime, or gain more than 100 XP with the weapon, you gain proficiency with it. You add your proficiency bonus to the Hit Roll.</p>

<h2>Gun Modifications</h2>
<p>Every firearm has three modification slots that can be filled with different gun modifications to improve its performance. Modifications grant specific mechanical bonuses, such as reducing Action Point (AP) costs, improving accuracy, increasing ammunition capacity, or improving reload speed. Modifications can be interchanged during downtime, allowing players to customize their firearms to suit their playstyle. Each modification provides a distinct benefit, giving players the freedom to build their weapons around their preferred approach to combat.</p>

<h2>Fishing</h2>

<p>Fishing in Dust &amp; Blood is resolved using three dice rolls. First, the player rolls a d20 to beat the Fishing DC assigned to the lake. The roll must be higher than the DC to successfully catch a fish; otherwise, the attempt fails. On a successful fishing check, the player rolls another d20 on the Freshwater Fish Table to determine which species they catch. The player then rolls a d12 on the Fish Size Table to determine the fish's size, ranging from tiny to trophy-sized. The fish's size determines its final selling price by multiplying its base price by the appropriate size modifier. The GM determines the fish's actual length and weight based on its species and size category.</p>

<h3>Fishing Rules</h3>

<p><strong>Requirement:</strong> You must have a fishing pole with bait or a lure.</p>

<ol>
<li>Roll a d20 against the lake's Fishing DC. You must roll higher than the DC to catch a fish.</li>
<li>If successful, roll a d20 on the Freshwater Fish Table.</li>
<li>Roll a d12 on the Fish Size Table to determine the fish's size category.</li>
<li>Multiply the fish's base selling price by its size modifier to determine its final value.</li>
<li>The GM determines the fish's actual length and weight based on its species and size category.</li>
</ol>

<p><strong>Example:</strong> You fish in a lake with a Fishing DC of 10 and roll a 15. You then roll a 9 on the Freshwater Fish Table, catching a Brown Trout. Your d12 size roll is 10, making it Very Large. Its base price of $0.45 is multiplied by 2, making it worth $0.90.</p>

<h2>Foraging</h2>

<p>Foraging in Dust &amp; Blood is resolved through three dice rolls. First, the player rolls a d20 to beat the Foraging DC assigned to the forest. The roll must exceed the DC to successfully find a plant; otherwise, the attempt fails. On a successful foraging check, the player rolls a d20 on the Plant Species Table to determine which herb they discover. Once the plant species has been determined, the player rolls a d4 on the Plant Purity Table to establish the purity of the herb they have gathered.</p>
